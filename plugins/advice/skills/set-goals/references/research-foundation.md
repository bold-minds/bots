# Goal Framework Research Foundation

The three-layer goal structure (Needle → Commitments → When-Thens) synthesizes evidence from multiple research traditions. This reference provides the basis for why each layer works, so the skill can explain the reasoning to the user when needed.

## Layer 1: The Needle (Lag Measure)

Based on **4DX (4 Disciplines of Execution)** — McChesney, Covey, Huling. The distinction between lag measures (outcomes you want) and lead measures (behaviors that drive outcomes) is the core insight. Track the lag measure to know if things are working. Act on the lead measures to make things work.

Also draws on **Locke & Latham's goal-setting theory** — specific, difficult goals outperform vague "do your best" goals across 1000+ studies. The needle must be specific enough to answer "did it move?" weekly.

## Layer 2: Commitments (Lead Measures)

Based on **pre-commitment / Ulysses contracts** — Schelling (Nobel laureate). Making decisions in a calm, rational state that bind your future self when temptation arrives. The commitment needs external structure because in-the-moment willpower is exactly what fails when the pattern runs — that premise is the whole framework.

**Accountability research** — sharing goals as commitments with check-ins increases follow-through (distinct from sharing goals as announcements, which can reduce effort via premature identity satisfaction — Gollwitzer, 2009).

**Loss aversion / stakes** — Kahneman & Tversky's prospect theory. Losses are ~2x as psychologically powerful as equivalent gains. Financial stakes (commitment-contract services — money staked on a goal) and social stakes (people counting on you) leverage this.

**Binary tracking** from **4DX** — the scoreboard must be simple. Kept or broken. No gray zone.

## Layer 3: When-Thens (Implementation Intentions)

Based on **Peter Gollwitzer's implementation intentions** research — medium-to-large effect size (d = 0.65) across 94 studies. "When X happens, I will do Y" creates strategic automaticity — the behavior becomes quasi-automatic, bypassing the need for in-the-moment willpower.

Most effective for people whose self-regulation is strong in some domains and weak in others — the exact shape constraint goals target.

## The WOOP Addition

Based on **Gabriele Oettingen's mental contrasting** — positive visualization alone undermines goal achievement by giving the brain premature reward. WOOP (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) combines positive visualization with obstacle identification, creating energization rather than complacency.

The obstacle step is critical: it forces naming the *internal* barrier (not "I don't have time" but "I feel anxious when I'm not working"). This self-honesty is what makes the subsequent implementation intention targeted.

## What The Research Says To Avoid

- **Streak tracking** — punishes rest and turns one missed day into abandoning the week; the chain becomes the goal instead of the behavior
- **SMART goals aimed at an already-dominant domain** — optimizing the strong domain deepens the imbalance the constraint goal exists to fix
- **Positive visualization without mental contrasting** — fantasizing about the outcome reduces the drive to create it (Oettingen)
- **Goal announcements without accountability structure** — premature identity satisfaction (Gollwitzer, 2009)
- **Productivity systems for personal life** — turns life into another project to optimize; boundary goals bound, they don't optimize

## Self-Determination Theory Context

**Deci & Ryan** — work satisfies autonomy, competence, and relatedness intensely. Non-work life may fail to satisfy these needs, making work the path of least resistance. Design non-work goals to include skill development (competence), social connection (relatedness), and personal choice (autonomy) — not just obligation.
